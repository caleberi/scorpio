## Come unto Christ
The first step when lifting 180-200kg weight is to find a spotter. Not just any spotter, but a spotter that is strong enough to hold you up and help you lift the weight. A spotter is someone that is there to help you lift the weight and to keep you safe. In same way, Christ is the spotter that I need to help me weight of my weaknesses, sin and burdens. Roman 5:8 says "8 But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us." meaning he has already lifted the weight of my sins and burdens on the cross. I just have to step out and ask him to spot me. 

One may think that well you are still carrying the weight, well that is not the point in this case. Carrying 200kg weight has the potential of killing you so when it offload to the spotter, you are safe and can continue with your workout.

Anyway, the first step is to come unto him and ask him to spot you.

## Promise of rest

The promise of rest. What is rest? In Hebrew, it means *menuchah* (מְנוּחָה) — from the root *nuach*, "to rest" or "to settle down." It's not just Sabbath, "stop working," which is a different word (*shabbat*, "to cease"). *Menuchah* is deeper than that. It's the settled, whole-hearted ease that comes *after* you stop striving — not just the absence of labour, but the presence of safety.

> I saw an instagram reel talking about Menuchah and I did not know I will be using it in a post at the time.

Think about it in gym terms. The moment I offload the bar to my spotter, something changes in my body. My legs stop shaking. My breathing slows. I'm not still under the bar — I've been let out from under it. That's *menuchah*. It's not merely "the set is over." It's "I am no longer at risk of being crushed."

That's the rest Christ is promising in verse 28. He's not saying "take a break and come back to the weight later." He's saying "give me the bar, and you will find *menuchah* for your soul" — a rest that isn't just a pause between sets, but a settled state you get to live in because someone stronger is now holding what was going to kill you.

This lines up with how the word is used elsewhere. The dove sent out from Noah's ark found "no rest" (*manoach*, same root) for its foot until it could land somewhere solid (Genesis 8:9) — rest as a place to set down, not just a moment of stillness. And when Israel finally entered the promised land, it's described as entering "the rest and the inheritance" (Deuteronomy 12:9) — rest as arrival, not just relief.

So when I was pouring my heart out after that pendulum squat set, thinking about how my weakness keeps beating me, the invitation wasn't "try harder next set." It was "hand me the bar." Rest isn't something I produce by finally getting strong enough to carry my own sin. It's something I receive the moment I stop trying to carry it and let the spotter take the weight.
